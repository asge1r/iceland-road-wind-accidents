# Investigation of the 2014 accident-count dip

Reviewed 15 September 2026. No scientific analysis or accident selection changed.

## Observed pattern

The existing `reports/main/tables/weather_coverage.csv` has 321 rural injury
accidents in 2013, 285 in 2014 and 377 in 2015. This is an annual count,
not an exposure-adjusted rate. The same table shows a decline across the
financial-crisis period and a low count in 2020; temporal coincidence alone
cannot establish an economic or pandemic effect.

## Explanations investigated and evidence

- **Reporting ownership and workflow.** The [ITA registry documentation](https://www.samgongustofa.is/umferd/tolfraedi/umferdarslys/um-slysaskra/)
  records the transfer to Samgöngustofa in 2013 and describes police reports
  as the injury-data source. It does not identify a 2014 break explaining this dip.
- **Definitions and data-source changes.** The [official 2014 accident report](https://www.samgongustofa.is/media/english/Slysaskyrsla2014umferd.pdf),
  published March 2015, pp. 5–7, describes earlier reporting changes: wider
  nationality coverage in 1992, digital transfer from 1998, geocoding from 2007,
  and supplementary reports from 2009. It discusses incomplete injury reporting.
  None establishes a 2014 discontinuity in this rural injury sample.
- **Adjacent-year documentation.** The [2015 report](https://www.samgongustofa.is/media/umferd/umferdaroryggi/Slysaskyrsla_2015.pdf),
  introduction, repeats this reporting history. It notes an unusually low fatality
  total in 2014, but fatalities are not the thesis's all-injury accident count.
- **Police reporting practice.** An [IRCA study of 2014 bicycle accidents](https://www.vegagerdin.is/vegagerdin/gagnasafn/skyrslur/utgefnar-rannsoknaskyrslur/umferd/2016-umferd/nakvaem-greining-hjolreidaslysa-slys-sem-urdu-2014)
  discusses improved reporting as one possible contributor to increased bicycle
  accident recording in 2013. This is a different subgroup and direction of change;
  it does not explain the rural all-injury decline in 2014.
- **Road-safety policy and national statistics.** Targeted official-source
  searches found policy context and national fatality statistics, but no evidence
  attributing the thesis's specific dip to a policy intervention. Repository
  documentation likewise provided no established explanation.

## Recommended and implemented wording

“The dip in 2014 is also visible. The official accident report documents the
reporting system and earlier changes but does not establish a 2014 reporting
change that explains this dip. These time patterns alone do not identify their
causes or separate changes in travel from changes in accident risk.”

The thesis cites the official 2014 report (`itaReport2014`, publication year 2015).

## Confidence and limits

High confidence in the plotted counts and the cited reporting history. No
established causal explanation was found in this bounded search. This is not
proof that no reporting change occurred. Registry staff would be the appropriate
source for any further explanation specific to this subset.
