The warm-weather excess is not confined to strong wind. At temperatures of at least 12$^{\circ}$C, accidents were more frequent
than expected from local weather frequency in every wind category.
However, the two highest wind categories contained too few accidents
for reliable comparisons. O/E is also above one immediately below freezing in every wind category (see Figure~\ref{fig:joint-weather-detail}).

Cold conditions do not show a uniform excess. Below $-6$$^{\circ}$C, O/E is 0.69 at wind speeds below 5 m/s but 1.94 at 15--20 m/s. This illustrates how the temperature pattern varies across wind categories without establishing a statistical interaction. Within the 15--20 m/s row, cells meeting the support rule range from O/E 0.93 at 3--6$^{\circ}$C to 2.06 immediately below freezing.

Six of the eight cells at wind speeds of at least 20 m/s have limited support. The largest O/E (7.63) occurs at 20 m/s or above and at least 12$^{\circ}$C, but represents only 2 observed accidents versus 0.26 expected. That extreme cell should not be interpreted as reliable evidence of an especially strong combined effect.
