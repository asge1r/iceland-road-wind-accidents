\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
This study examines whether rural injury accidents in Iceland occur disproportionately
during strong wind. Accident records from 2007--2025 were linked to nearby
weather observations. The primary analysis compared observed accident counts
with those expected from the local frequency of each weather condition. Two
further analyses examined the role of traffic: an approximate adjustment for
changes in traffic during adverse weather, and a separate comparison of
accident rates using daily traffic counts and estimated distance travelled.
Mean wind was the primary weather measure; gust and temperature provided
complementary comparisons.

Strong wind was over-represented at accident times. At mean wind speeds of at
least 20 m/s, the observed-to-expected accident ratio (O/E) was 2.15.
Counters showed lower traffic in strong wind. Accounting approximately for
this reduction strengthened rather than removed
this pattern. The separate traffic-based rate analysis pointed in the same
direction, although its highest-wind intervals contained few accidents.
Gust showed a similar pattern, while temperature associations were less
consistent across methods.

The findings support an association between strong wind and rural injury
accidents, not a causal effect. Weather observations cannot fully describe
conditions on the road, and the traffic analyses rely on selective counter
coverage and estimated exposure within each day. Hourly traffic data with
broader geographic coverage would be needed for a direct national estimate
of accident risk per distance travelled.

Rannsóknin fjallar um tengsl veðurs og umferðarslysa með meiðslum í dreifbýli
á Íslandi á árunum 2007--2025. Kannað var hvort slys væru hlutfallslega
algengari í miklum vindi en við önnur veðurskilyrði. Slysaskrár voru tengdar
veðurmælingum frá nálægum stöðvum og fjöldi slysa borinn saman við þann
fjölda sem vænta mætti miðað við hversu oft viðkomandi veður kom fyrir á
hverjum stað. Einnig var tekið tillit til þess að umferð getur minnkað í
slæmu veðri. Annars vegar var samanburðurinn leiðréttur með hliðsjón af
gögnum um daglega umferð og hins vegar var slysatíðni metin miðað við áætlaðan
akstur á vegum þar sem umferðarteljarar voru til staðar.

Slys reyndust hlutfallslega algengari í miklum vindi. Við meðalvindhraða
20 m/s eða meiri var hlutfall skráðs og væntanlegs fjölda slysa 2,15.
